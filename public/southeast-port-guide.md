## Port 1: Port of Mobile, Alabama

**Address:** 201 S Water St, Mobile, AL 36602

### Drive Times
- Birmingham: 4.5 hours
- Huntsville: 4 hours
- Atlanta: 5.5 hours
- Nashville: 5.5 hours
- Chattanooga: 5.5 hours
- Knoxville: 7 hours
- Memphis: 7.5 hours
- Savannah: 8 hours

### Cruise Lines
**Carnival Cruise Line** — the Carnival Spirit sails year-round.

### Itineraries
4- to 7-night Western Caribbean sailings. Typical ports: Cozumel, Costa Maya, Progreso, Mahogany Bay (Roatán).

### Parking
~$18/night at the port's official garage. Pre-pay online for a small discount.

### Recommended Pre-Cruise Hotel
**Renaissance Mobile Riverview Plaza** — walking distance to the cruise terminal, great restaurant on-site, and reasonable "park and cruise" packages that throw in a week of parking with your room night.

### Tim's Take
Mobile is the best-kept secret on this list. It's often $200–$500 cheaper per cabin than the same Carnival itinerary out of New Orleans or Tampa, drive-accessible for almost every Southeast city, and the port itself is small and painless. If you're in Alabama, Tennessee, or Georgia and haven't considered Mobile, you're leaving money on the table.

**Downside:** only one ship, so itinerary variety is limited. Book early for peak seasons.

## Port 2: New Orleans, Louisiana

**Addresses:** Julia Street Cruise Terminal & Erato Street Cruise Terminal (both at the foot of the CBD)

### Drive Times
- Birmingham: 6 hours
- Memphis: 6 hours
- Huntsville: 6 hours
- Atlanta: 7 hours
- Nashville: 8 hours
- Chattanooga: 8.5 hours
- Savannah: 9 hours

### Cruise Lines
**Norwegian, Carnival, Royal Caribbean** — rotating ships throughout the year.

### Itineraries
5- to 7-night Western Caribbean. Typical ports: Cozumel, Costa Maya, Roatán, Harvest Caye (Belize).

### Parking
~$25/night at the official Port of New Orleans garages. Private lots off-site run $12–$18/night with shuttle service.

### Recommended Pre-Cruise Hotel
**Hilton New Orleans Riverside** — directly connected to the Julia Street terminal, full French Quarter access, and arguably the most convenient pre-cruise stay in any US port.

### Tim's Take
New Orleans is the play if you want a cruise **plus** a destination city experience without flying. Spend two nights on Bourbon Street, board a ship Sunday, and you've stacked two vacations into one. Pricing is usually a tick higher than Mobile but you get bigger ships and more itinerary variety.

**Downside:** traffic in and out of downtown NOLA can be rough on sail day. Build in a buffer or stay downtown the night before.

## Port 3: Jacksonville, Florida

**Address:** JAXPORT Cruise Terminal, 9810 August Dr, Jacksonville, FL 32226

### Drive Times
- Savannah: 2 hours
- Atlanta: 5.5 hours
- Chattanooga: 7 hours
- Birmingham: 8 hours
- Nashville: 9.5 hours
- Huntsville: 8 hours

### Cruise Lines
**Carnival Cruise Line** — Carnival Elation (being replaced on a rolling basis; check current deployment).

### Itineraries
4- to 5-night Bahamas sailings. Typical ports: Nassau, Princess Cays, Half Moon Cay.

### Parking
~$17/night. Often included in park-and-cruise hotel packages.

### Recommended Pre-Cruise Hotel
**Four Points by Sheraton Jacksonville Baymeadows** or any park-and-cruise hotel near the airport. Most offer free port shuttle + 5 days of free parking bundled with your room night — often cheaper than parking at the port alone.

### Tim's Take
Jacksonville is the ultimate "quick getaway" cruise port for the Southeast. Short itineraries, low prices, minimal logistics. If you're in Savannah or South Georgia, it's a no-brainer. For Atlanta and further north, it's a comfortable day drive for a family willing to leave early Friday morning.

**Downside:** limited ship selection and only Bahamas itineraries. Not your port for a 10-night Caribbean.

## Port 4: Port Canaveral, Florida

**Address:** Cruise Terminals 1, 3, 5, 8, 10 (different terminals for different cruise lines)

### Drive Times
- Savannah: 4.5 hours
- Atlanta: 7 hours
- Chattanooga: 9 hours
- Birmingham: 9.5 hours
- Nashville: 11 hours (usually fly)
- Knoxville: 9.5 hours
- Memphis: fly

### Cruise Lines
**Disney Cruise Line, Carnival, Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, MSC** — one of the busiest ports in the country with a huge range of ships.

### Itineraries
3- to 7-night Bahamas and Caribbean. Home of Disney's Castaway Cay and Lookout Cay stops; Royal Caribbean's Perfect Day at CocoCay.

### Parking
~$17–$20/night at the port. Surrounding off-site lots offer shuttle service at similar rates.

### Recommended Pre-Cruise Hotel
**Residence Inn Cape Canaveral Cocoa Beach** for families (suites + free breakfast + port shuttle) or **Hilton Cocoa Beach Oceanfront** if you want beach time before the cruise.

### Tim's Take
If you have kids, Port Canaveral is probably your port. Disney Cruise Line's fleet is here, Royal Caribbean's CocoCay itineraries launch from here, and the 3-night and 4-night options make it the easiest "add a cruise to our Orlando trip" port in the country.

**Downside:** it's popular, which means peak-season pricing and more crowded terminals.

## Port 5: Tampa, Florida

**Address:** Port Tampa Bay, 815 Channelside Dr, Tampa, FL 33602

### Drive Times
- Atlanta: 7 hours
- Savannah: 7 hours
- Chattanooga: 9 hours
- Birmingham: 10 hours
- Knoxville: 9.5 hours (direct flight from TYS is usually better)
- Nashville: 11 hours (fly)

### Cruise Lines
**Carnival, Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, Holland America** (seasonal).

### Itineraries
4- to 7-night Western Caribbean. Typical ports: Cozumel, Grand Cayman, Costa Maya.

### Parking
~$15/night at the port — one of the cheaper options in Florida.

### Recommended Pre-Cruise Hotel
**Embassy Suites by Hilton Tampa Downtown Convention Center** — walking distance to the terminal, free breakfast, and consistently strong reviews.

### Tim's Take
Tampa is one of the most underrated ports on this list. Less crowded than Miami, easier to get in and out of than Fort Lauderdale, and the drive down I-75 from Atlanta is one of the more relaxed Florida drives. Great option if you want a Western Caribbean itinerary without the Mobile/NOLA Gulf Coast experience.

**Downside:** ship clearance under the Sunshine Skyway Bridge limits which ships can sail from Tampa, so you won't see the newest mega-ships here.

## Port 6: Port Everglades (Fort Lauderdale)

**Address:** 1850 Eller Dr, Fort Lauderdale, FL 33316

### Drive Times
- Savannah: 9 hours
- Atlanta: 10 hours
- Everyone else: fly.

### Cruise Lines
**Princess, Holland America, Celebrity, Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, Cunard, Silversea**. Home of Allure of the Seas.

### Itineraries
Everything from 3-night Bahamas to 14-night Caribbean and transatlantic repositioning cruises. Widest selection of longer itineraries of any Southeast-accessible port.

### Parking
~$22/night at the port. Off-site lots run $10–$15/night with shuttles.

### Recommended Pre-Cruise Hotel
**Embassy Suites by Hilton Fort Lauderdale 17th Street** — 10 minutes from the port, free shuttle, suites for families.

### Tim's Take
Fort Lauderdale is Miami's quieter, more civilized sibling. Better if you want longer itineraries (10, 11, 14 nights) or premium lines like Celebrity and Princess. For Southeast travelers, it's a fly-in port — the 9+ hour drive doesn't make sense unless you're stacking a South Florida vacation onto the cruise.

**Downside:** flights into FLL from the Southeast are often more expensive than Miami. Always price both.

## Port 7: Port of Miami

**Address:** PortMiami, 1015 N America Way, Miami, FL 33132

### Drive Times
Everyone: fly. Direct flights from ATL, BNA, BHM, MEM, TYS, and SAV.

### Cruise Lines
**Royal Caribbean, Carnival, MSC, Virgin Voyages, Celebrity, Norwegian, Disney, Oceania, Regent, Seabourn** — more cruise lines than any other US port.

### Itineraries
Everything. 3-night weekenders to 14-night Southern Caribbean. Home of Royal Caribbean's Icon of the Seas and the world's largest cruise ships.

### Parking
~$26/night at the port. Off-site options (MIA Cruise Parking, Park 'N Go) run $12–$18/night with shuttles.

### Recommended Pre-Cruise Hotel
**InterContinental Miami** — downtown, walkable to the port, true city experience. Or **Hyatt Centric Las Olas Fort Lauderdale** if you want a quieter pre-cruise night and can Uber/shuttle over in the morning.

### Tim's Take
Miami is where you go for the newest ships. If you want Icon of the Seas, Utopia of the Seas, the new Virgin Voyages ships, or any Royal Caribbean mega-ship — it's here. Always fly in, always stay one night pre-cruise (flight delays are a real risk), and book transfers in advance.

**Downside:** peak-season traffic from MIA to the port can easily add 45 minutes. Build in a real buffer.

## Flying vs. Driving: The Real Math

Here's the honest framework:

**Drive if:**
- The port is under 6 hours from your house
- You're a family of 4 or more (baggage fees add up fast)
- You want flexibility on your arrival time
- You're bringing lots of luggage or special gear

**Fly if:**
- The port is 8+ hours away
- You can get a direct flight for under $300/person round-trip
- You're a couple or small group
- Your departure day is midweek (flights are cheaper)

**The middle zone (6–8 hours) is a real toss-up.** Once you add gas ($100–$150 round-trip), a pre-cruise hotel night ($150–$250), and port parking for a week ($150), driving often costs nearly as much as flying — without the speed.

## The Five Biggest Mistakes Southeast Cruisers Make

**1. Booking the cruise line's "posted" price.**
Cruise line websites almost never show you the best rate. Travel advisors have access to promotions, group amenity points, and onboard credit stacks that the public never sees. This alone can save you $200–$800 per cabin.

**2. Arriving the morning of the cruise.**
One delayed flight, one traffic jam on I-75, and your cruise sails without you. The cruise line doesn't refund you, and travel insurance coverage varies. **Always arrive the day before** for any cruise requiring a flight.

**3. Picking the wrong cabin category.**
The difference between an oceanview and a balcony is often only $200–$400 per person — and it completely changes the experience. On the flip side, an interior cabin on a 3-night Bahamas cruise (where you'll be on deck the whole time anyway) is a smart save.

**4. Booking the first itinerary that catches your eye.**
The same cruise line, same ship, same ports — sailing two weeks earlier or later — can be $500+ cheaper. Flexibility on dates is the single biggest lever for a better price.

**5. Skipping travel insurance.**
Especially for Caribbean cruises during hurricane season. "Cancel for any reason" policies exist and they're worth it for high-value trips. We help every client evaluate whether it makes sense for their specific trip.
